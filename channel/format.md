# Episode Format

## Runtime

**50–65 seconds.** Shorts-first. No episode needs to be longer than one rule.

## Beat sheet

| Beat | Time | Content | Who speaks |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1. Cold open | 0:00–0:10 | Chacha in a location. States the rule and the consequence. | Chacha |
| 2. Transition | 0:10–0:12 | He opens the rulebook. Camera pushes into the page. | None |
| 3. Story — normal | 0:12–0:18 | The protagonist in an ordinary situation. Establish the want. | Characters |
| 4. Story — breach | 0:18–0:28 | The sympathetic reason to break the rule. The choice is made. | Characters |
| 5. Story — escalation | 0:28–0:40 | The consequence begins. The protagonist tries to undo it. | Characters |
| 6. Story — twist | 0:40–0:50 | The rule proves itself. The cost lands. | Characters |
| 7. Return | 0:50–0:58 | Chacha delivers the verdict. Closes the book. | Chacha |
| 8. Final image | 0:58–1:00 | One silent shot from the story. | None |

## The cold open

Chacha is already in the location. No walking in, no setup. He looks at the
viewer and speaks as if continuing a conversation.

**Template:**

> "Suno meri baat. [Rule.] Agar tum ne aisa kiya, [consequence]. Yeh kahani
> uski hai jo nahi mana."

Then he opens the book.

The rule must be stated once, clearly. The consequence must be one sentence. No
extra lore in the cold open.

## The transition

A physical action into the book:

- He opens the leather cover.
- The camera pushes into the page.
- The page texture becomes the first shot of the story (a wall, a road, a bedsheet).

No magical effect. No glowing. The book is a door, not a spell.

## The story

**Rules of the story:**

1. **No narrator.** Every line is spoken by a character in the scene.
2. **2–4 speaking characters.** Each one wants something in the scene.
3. **Dialogue carries the story.** Visuals carry the dread. Nobody explains the rule.
4. **No Chacha.** He is not in the story, not in a window, not in a reflection.
5. **One location or two adjacent locations.** Keep it producible.
6. **The breach is sympathetic.** Love, fear, politeness, need, pride — not stupidity.
7. **The consequence fits the breach.** The bill is shaped like the choice.
8. **No explanation of the entity.** Show the cost; leave the thing itself off-screen
   or partially seen.
9. **The last story shot is silent.** One image that stays.

**Scene structure:**

- Scene 1: Normal. The character wants something small and human.
- Scene 2: The rule is in the way. The character chooses to break it for a reason
  the audience understands.
- Scene 3: The first consequence. They try to fix it.
- Scene 4: The twist. The rule was right. The cost lands.
- Final image: the image the viewer remembers.

## The return

Chacha is in the same location and the same shot as the cold open. He closes the
book and delivers the verdict.

**Template:**

> "[Verdict.] Rule yaad rakhna."

The verdict is not a proverb. It is a specific sentence about what the character
got wrong. It can be dry, but it is never a joke at the victim's expense.

## The final image

Return to the story's last shot for two seconds. No music sting. No jump scare. The
silence is the scare.

## Dialogue rules

- Maximum two lines per character per scene.
- No exposition dumps. If a fact matters, let a character act on it.
- Use concrete nouns: *the door, the bus, the ring, the photo.*
- Ordinary Urdu, not literary Urdu. Chacha can be a little formal; the story
  characters speak like real people.
- Silence is allowed. A held look is a line.

## Production notes

- Chacha's frame is a reusable set per location. One setup, two angles.
- The story can use 2–3 simple sets. Keep the camera close.
- Voice cast: 4–6 recurring voice actors can cover the whole catalog. See
  `series/chacha-mashwara/bible.md` for voice archetypes.
- The rulebook is the only prop Chacha needs.

## What makes an episode fail

- Chacha narrates the story.
- The story explains the rule instead of proving it.
- The breach is stupid rather than sympathetic.
- The ending is ambiguous about the *event* instead of the *image*.
- A joke undercuts the dread.
- The consequence does not fit the breach.
