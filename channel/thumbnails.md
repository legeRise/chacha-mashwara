# Thumbnails

## The job

A thumbnail has one job: make the rule feel like a warning about something in the
viewer's own life. Not "look at this monster." **"Look at this thing you have done."**

## The formula

**Chacha corner mark + the object/place + 2–5 words of rule text.**

- **Chacha corner mark:** chest-up, three-quarter turn, warm lamplight, rulebook
  visible. Same crop every time. It is the brand stamp.
- **The object/place:** the specific thing the rule is about — a door, a bus seat,
  a mirror, a wedding chair, a phone. Shot close, slightly off-center.
- **Text:** 2–5 words, heavy sans, warm cream on deep green or oxblood. Never a
  full sentence.

## Composition rotation

Do not use the same composition twice in a row. Rotate:

1. **Object close-up** — the rule object fills the frame.
2. **Doorway/threshold** — the viewer is about to step through.
3. **Face, half-lit** — a character looking at something off-screen.
4. **Empty room** — the absence is the threat.
5. **POV** — the viewer's own hands/feet in the scene.
6. **Silhouette** — a shape where a shape should not be.
7. **Chacha + object** — Chacha holding or standing beside the object.

Log the composition per episode in `ledger/episodes.csv`. No composition more than
twice in any 10 episodes.

## Palette

- Warm cream: `#F2E6D0`
- Deep green: `#1E3A2F`
- Oxblood: `#5A1E1E`
- Lamp yellow: `#E8B45A`
- Ink: `#14100E`

No neon. No blue-purple "YouTube horror" gradient. The palette should feel like a
warm room with one dark corner.

## Typography

- Heavy grotesque or condensed sans.
- 2–5 words, all caps, tight leading.
- Cream text on dark green/oxblood, or dark ink on cream.
- Text in the lower third or upper third — never over the object's face.
- Chacha's corner mark stays clear of the text.

## Hard NOs

- No gore.
- No screaming open-mouth faces.
- No red eyes, no fangs, no generic ghost girls.
- No arrows, circles, or "SHOCKING" stickers.
- No text that repeats the title word-for-word at full length.
- No real people's faces.

## Thumbnail promise

Write the promise in the ledger:

> "If you answer the door after midnight, the voice outside will learn yours."

The thumbnail should make the viewer *feel* that promise, not read the whole story.
If the thumbnail promises a different story than the episode delivers, the episode
is off-brand even if the story is good.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Is the rule object visible and specific?
- [ ] Is Chacha's corner mark present?
- [ ] Is the composition different from the last two episodes?
- [ ] Is the text 2–5 words?
- [ ] Does it feel like a warning, not a movie poster?
- [ ] Would a viewer understand the danger in one second?
