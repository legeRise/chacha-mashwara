# Story Formula

## The engine

> **Rule → human reason → breach → consequence → twist → final image → verdict.**

The rule is stated by Chacha. Everything after the transition is the story.

## The five story beats

| Beat | Duration | Job | Test |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1. Normal | 5–6s | Show a small, human want. | Do we like this person? |
| 2. Breach | 8–10s | Give a sympathetic reason to break the rule. | Would I have done it? |
| 3. Escalation | 10–12s | The first consequence; they try to undo it. | Is the cost rising? |
| 4. Twist | 8–10s | The rule proves itself; the cost lands. | Did the promise pay off? |
| 5. Final image | 2–3s | One silent shot. | Is this the image they remember? |

## The sympathy rule

The breach must come from one of these:

- **Love** — they are protecting someone.
- **Fear** — they are afraid of looking foolish or weak.
- **Politeness** — they cannot say no.
- **Need** — they need money, shelter, sleep, medicine.
- **Pride** — they want to be seen as brave or modern.
- **Hope** — they believe there is a happy ending.

If the reason is "they were curious," the story is not ready. Curiosity is not a
reason; it is a plot device.

## The irony rule

The consequence must fit the breach:

| Breach | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Let a stranger in because you trust their voice. | The stranger leaves with your voice. |
| Count the guests to show off your hosting. | The house counts one extra. |
| Take the last seat because you are tired. | The seat was being saved for someone. |
| Lie at a funeral to spare someone's feelings. | The dead hear the lie. |
| Keep a photo of someone who left. | They come back for it. |

The consequence is not random. It is the shape of the mistake, turned inside out.

## The no-explanation rule

Never explain:

- what the entity is,
- where it came from,
- what it wants,
- how to defeat it.

The rule is the only explanation. The story shows the cost. The audience supplies
the fear.

## The one-location rule

Most stories should happen in one location, or two adjacent ones:

- a flat and its corridor,
- a bus and the road outside,
- a wedding hall and the bride's room,
- a shop and the alley behind it.

If the story needs three locations, it is probably two rules fighting.

## The dialogue rule

- No narration. Characters speak to each other.
- Each character wants something in the scene.
- Maximum two lines per character per scene.
- No exposition. If a fact matters, let someone act on it.
- Use ordinary Urdu. No poetry.
- Silence is allowed.

## The cast rule

- 2–4 speaking roles.
- Each role has a distinct voice and a distinct want.
- No voice actor plays two roles in the same episode.
- A crowd is a sound, not a character.

## The final image

The last story shot is:

- silent,
- held for two seconds,
- the visual proof of the rule,
- the image on the thumbnail (or its opposite).

**Examples:**

- The open door, the corridor empty.
- A second shadow on the wall, slightly behind the first.
- A chair pulled out at a table set for two.
- The bus seat still warm, the road empty.
- A photo with one more person in it than before.

## The verdict

Chacha's last line names the mistake, not the monster. It should make the viewer
replay the story in their head.

**Bad:** "So you see, you should always listen to your elders."
**Good:** "Tum ne awaaz pehchani, darwaza nahi." — You recognized the voice, not
the door.

## The test pass

Before a script is approved:

- [ ] The rule is stated in the first 10 seconds.
- [ ] The breach is sympathetic.
- [ ] The consequence fits the breach.
- [ ] Chacha never enters the story.
- [ ] There is no narrator.
- [ ] The twist pays off the rule, not a different rule.
- [ ] The final image is silent and specific.
- [ ] The verdict is one sentence.
- [ ] The whole thing fits 50–65 seconds.
