# Chacha Mashwara — Series Bible

## Who he is

**Chacha Mashwara** is a Pakistani uncle in his late fifties. He is the only
recurring character in the series. Every story is told *to* him, not *by* him: he
opens the frame, states the rule, and then the characters live the story in their
own voices. He returns at the end to close it.

He is not a ghost, a saint, or a detective. He is a man who has collected warnings
his whole life and believes they are a form of mercy.

## Appearance

- Round soft belly, rounded shoulders.
- Big grey moustache, neatly kept.
- Kind, mischievous eyes behind thin-rimmed round glasses.
- Deep smile lines.
- Crisp white shalwar kameez.
- Deep green waistcoat with gold buttons.
- Cream prayer cap.
- One ring on one finger.
- Carries a worn leather rulebook, always.

## Voice

- Warm Urdu, measured, unhurried.
- Low and close, as if speaking to one person across a table.
- A faint everyday lilt — not theatrical, not a "wise old man" performance.
- Pauses before the rule and before the verdict.
- Never shouts. Never pleads. Never explains the supernatural.

**Voice archetype for casting:** a real uncle, not a narrator. Think of the person
at a family gathering who tells the story everyone goes quiet for.

## Mannerisms

- Strokes the moustache once before stating the rule.
- Opens the rulebook with one hand.
- Runs a thumb along the page edge before the transition.
- Closes the book and taps the cover twice at the end.
- Looks at the viewer, not the camera lens — as if the viewer is sitting across
  from him.

## Psychology

- He believes rules are not cages; they are railings.
- He has seen what happens when people mistake confidence for safety.
- He never enjoys the story. He tells it because he has to.
- He is warm to the viewer, but there is a sadness underneath.
- He never says "I told you so" with pleasure. The verdict is a sigh, not a jab.

## What he never does

- He never narrates the story.
- He never appears inside the story — not as a passerby, a reflection, a voice on
  the phone, or a photo on the wall.
- He never explains what the entity is.
- He never uses religion as a threat or a prop.
- He never jokes about the dead or the victim.
- He never gives more than one rule per episode.

## The frame

**Cold open:** Chacha is already in a location. The rulebook is closed in his lap or
on the table. He states the rule and the consequence. He opens the book.

**Return:** The same shot. He closes the book, delivers the verdict, taps the cover
twice.

**The transition:** the camera pushes into the page. The page's texture becomes the
first image of the story. No glow, no magic. The book is a door, not a spell.

## The rulebook

- Worn brown leather, no title on the cover.
- Pages in many different hands, some very old.
- Each page holds one rule and one story.
- Chacha writes the new rule into the book at the end of each episode — but the
  camera never shows him writing. We only see the closed book.
- The last page is blank. It has been blank for a long time.

## The empty chair

In Chacha's home there is an empty chair at the table. It is never explained. It is
never used as a jump scare. It is simply there. One day, a story will explain it.
Not yet.

## Voice cast

The story is performed by a rotating ensemble of voice actors. Chacha is the only
constant. The catalog uses role labels, not actor names.

**Core archetypes:**

- **The Young Man** — earnest, a little proud, wants to be taken seriously.
- **The Young Woman** — watchful, practical, often the first to notice.
- **The Mother** — warm, tired, protective, superstitious in a practical way.
- **The Father** — authoritative, skeptical, soft underneath.
- **The Elder** — slow, deliberate, knows more than he says.
- **The Child** — direct, unnerving, honest.
- **The Stranger** — polite, slightly too calm.
- **The Worker** — driver, guard, servant, shopkeeper; sees everything, says little.
- **The Bride/Groom** — hopeful, distracted, pulled between families.
- **The Neighbor** — nosy, helpful, carries the rumor.

Casting rule: each episode uses **2–4 voices**, and no voice plays two characters in
the same episode.

## The story characters

They are one-offs. They do not return. They do not know Chacha. They are ordinary
people with a small want and a human reason to break the rule.

The audience should never think: *"This is the episode where the hero wins."* The
audience should think: *"I would have done the same thing."*

## The verdict

The verdict is one sentence, spoken by Chacha, that names the mistake — not the
monster.

**Examples:**

- "Tum ne awaaz pehchani, darwaza nahi." — You recognized the voice, not the door.
- "Mehman ko naam dena tha, jagah nahi." — You gave the guest a name, not a place.
- "Raat ko sunna nahi tha; subah dekhna tha." — You should have listened in the
  morning, not at night.

The verdict never repeats a proverb. It is specific to the story.

## The Urdu voice

- Chacha speaks formal but simple Urdu.
- Story characters speak everyday Urdu, with occasional Punjabi words where natural.
- No literary poetry. No sermons.
- The rule and the verdict are the only lines allowed to sound "written."

## Brand boundaries

- No gore.
- No religious disrespect.
- No real names.
- No sexual content.
- No children harmed on screen.
- No comedy episodes.
- No crossover with the old lateefa/Pappu videos.

## The long arc (slow-burn, optional)

Chacha is collecting rules because he once broke one. The empty chair is the person
he could not warn in time. The last page of the rulebook is blank because his own
rule has not been written yet.

This is never the subject of an episode. It is only a shadow. One or two frames a
season are enough.
