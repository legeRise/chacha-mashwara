# Lore

## The rulebook

A worn brown leather book with no title on the cover. It is older than Chacha. The
pages are in many hands: some careful, some hurried, some almost illegible. Each
page holds one rule and one story. Some pages are crossed out. Chacha never explains
who crossed them out.

The book is not magic. It does not glow, whisper, or open by itself. It is a record.
That is what makes it frightening.

At the end of every episode, the camera holds on the closed book. We never see
Chacha write. We only see that the book is a little thicker than before.

## The last page

The last page is blank. It has been blank for as long as Chacha can remember. He
does not know what rule belongs there. The audience should not know either.

Do not resolve this in the first year. It is a horizon, not a plot.

## The empty chair

In Chacha's home there is an empty chair at the table. It is never explained, never
used for a jump scare, and never mentioned by Chacha. It is simply there in the
background of one or two frames a season.

One day, a story will explain it. That story is not in the first 1,000 rules.

## Why Chacha collects rules

He once broke one. He does not say which. The consequences are not shown. The
sadness in his manner is the only evidence.

The audience can infer: the empty chair is connected. Chacha's warmth toward
strangers is a form of apology. He warns people because he cannot warn the person
who mattered most.

Never confirm this in dialogue. Let it live in the performance.

## The city

Unnamed. A dense, ordinary Pakistani city with buses, rooftops, wedding halls,
tailor shops, clinics, and a dry river bridge. It has no map. Landmarks recur:
the tea stall before dawn, the bus stop after midnight, the bridge over the dry
river. The city is not haunted. It is full of people who do not listen.

## The supernatural

- Implied, never explained.
- No chosen one, no prophecy, no secret society.
- No religious explanation. No rituals, no possessed objects that need a ceremony.
- The entity is usually off-screen, partial, or ordinary. A voice. A shape in a
  doorway. A second set of footprints. A face that is almost familiar.
- The rule is the only explanation the audience gets. That is the point.

## The rules of the lore

1. Chacha never enters a story.
2. Chacha never explains the supernatural.
3. The rulebook never does anything magical.
4. The empty chair is never a jump scare.
5. The last page is never shown clearly.
6. No episode ends with "it was all a dream."
7. No episode reveals that Chacha was the monster.
8. The stories are not connected. The frame is the only continuity.

## The slow-burn

One frame a season is enough:

- The empty chair, slightly moved.
- A page in the book in Chacha's own handwriting — but we cannot read it.
- Chacha looking at the last page before closing the book.
- A child in a story who looks like someone Chacha once knew.

Never explain. Never wink at the audience. The lore is a pressure, not a puzzle.
