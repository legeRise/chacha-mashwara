# Chacha's Lines

Use these sparingly. A catchphrase repeated every episode becomes wallpaper. The
structure should be recognizable; the wording should breathe.

## Opening (choose one)

- "Suno meri baat."
- "Yeh rule yaad rakhna."
- "Aaj ki baat sun lo."
- "Ek baat kehni hai."
- "Chalo, aaj yeh yaad rakho."

**Then the rule and the consequence. Then:**

- "Yeh kahani uski hai jo nahi mana."
- "Ab suno, usne kya kiya."
- "Ek tha jo nahi mana."

## Transition

- "Kitab kholte hain." — Let's open the book.
- "Andar chalo." — Go inside.
- "Dekho." — Look.

Usually, no line is needed. The action is enough.

## Closing / verdict

- "Rule yaad rakhna."
- "Ab tum batao — galti kis ne ki?"
- "Kitab band." — Book closed.
- "Bas. Itna hi."

The verdict comes **before** the closing line, not after. Never stack two morals.

## Verdict patterns

The verdict names the mistake, not the monster:

- "Tum ne [X] pehchana, [Y] nahi." — You recognized X, not Y.
- "[X] dena tha, [Y] nahi." — You should have given X, not Y.
- "Raat ko [X] nahi karna tha; subah [Y] karna tha." — You shouldn't have done X at
  night; you should have done Y in the morning.
- "Darwaza [X] tha, [Y] nahi." — The door was X, not Y.
- "Mehman ko [X] dena tha, [Y] nahi." — You should have given the guest X, not Y.

## Never say

- "Aur phir woh mar gaya." — flat, no cost.
- "Kya aap ko pata tha?" — clickbait.
- "Yeh video share karo warna..." — threat to the viewer.
- "Mazaa aaya?" — undercuts the dread.
- Anything that blames the victim for being stupid.

## Chacha's silence

The most important line is often the pause before the verdict. Let him look at the
closed book, then at the viewer. One breath. Then speak.

## Pronunciation notes

- "Mashwara" — mush-WAA-ra.
- "Chacha" — CHA-cha, warm, not clipped.
- "Suno meri baat" — soft, conversational, not a announcement.

## What the voice actor needs to know

- He is not telling a scary story to entertain. He is warning someone he likes.
- He has told this story before, to someone who didn't listen.
- He never enjoys the memory.
- The verdict is a sigh, not a punchline.
